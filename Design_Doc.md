# Gregpack Design Manifesto
## Introduction
This modpack is the culmination of (relatively few) years of modded Minecraft experience, and the desire to get a specific experience out of that time that up to this point has been missing from the larger modpack space.
GregTech has become a favorite mod of mine for its long term progression and reward. Playing the mod and engaging with it on a deeper level has been some of the most fun I have had playing Minecraft, but no modpack I have played up to this point has *quite* hit the particular feelings that I want.
To list off a few:
- **Nomifactory CEu** remains the single GregTech pack I have played to completion. Its focus on speedy progression on quality of life make it a joy to play for just about any type of player, but due to being a pack that puts the factory before everything else, it unfortunately foregoes many of the vanilla survival gameplay elements and mechanics to achieve that goal. This is obviously not a flaw of the pack, just the experience that it decided to be designed for.
- **StaTech Industry**, while not a GregTech pack, somewhat occupies a similar design space. Particularly as of the release of the 2.0 version of that modpack, it retains most vanilla elements for the sake of retaining them, but otherwise builds a very tight and focused factory building experience that is, in my opinion, unrivaled in pure factory gameplay. Modern Industrialization, unlike GregTech, focuses on being a standalone experience that can be dropped into any modpack without much worry for conflicting mechanics. Every problem that this mod presents you can be solved through the mod itself, it does not require secondary mods like AE2 to augment what already exists. It focuses entirely on *consistent, passive* automation and punishes you for not engaging with the mod in that way.
- **GregTech: New Horizons**, being the defacto GregTech modpack, colors a lot of the general perception of the mod with its design decisions. It demands a *lot* of time and effort, it forces alot of mods and mechanics on you that you may not want to engage with, it has comical infrastructure requirements, all for less reward than some more streamlined packs. In spite of this, it creates a genuinely really solid survival experience that combines survival Minecraft with how I believe GregTech "should" be, at least in some aspects.
- **Technological Journey** can be seen as similar to Nomifactory in that it has a large factory emphasis with little (though not none in this case) emphasis on the survival aspect of the game. It differs in that it has less of a focus on secondary mods such as EnderIO or Thermal, and instead puts all of its cards into the old Gregicality addon, which expanded GregTech progression past UHV. It can be seen as artificially extending the playtime of the game, but I believe that extra content can really tie together the whole experience and serve as an incredible capstone to a GregTech world. My major qualm with TJ is the large amount of QOL, such as giving you instamining hammers and spades out of the gate and giving you multiple items that you cannot obtain at that point in progression through the questbook (TIAB + miner with solar panel is an undeniably really fun exploit but it spits in the face of legitimate, unimpeded progression). Again, not a problem with the pack, just part of what it tries to achieve.

Every one of these modpacks are to be drawn from to create one ideal GregTech modpack to my tastes. Up to this point, there is no GregTech modpack that
- does not waste obnoxious amounts of your time, **while** not being overbearingly easy to the point of being potentially boring,
- does not require substantial use of other mods to finish within a reasonable time frame,
- has survival elements that are present but not overbearing,
- has post UHV content,


all in one package.

This is the conundrum that I hope the solve with this modpack. I do not claim or even hope that it will surpass any of the listed packs above in sheer quality (it probably wont), but it *will* be a curated experience to suit my specific tastes, and I hope that others can enjoy the experience and help me improve it. Nothing, and i mean **nothing** in this document is set in stone, every part of it is subject to change. However, what is currently here will guide development going forward.
With that, lets highlight some
## Specific Changes of Note
**Most "harder" recipe configs are enabled.** This includes nerfed wood recipes, redstone recipes, no crafting grid compressing recipes, etc. Any config that changes vanilla items is on. (VERY subject to change)\
The hard lathe recipes for rods are enabled (1 rod 2 small dust rather than 2 rods). Depending on how long things generally take, I might revert this one, but as of right now I like the idea of having a small "rod processing line", where you are encouraged to reprocess the small dust back into ingots. 

**Vanilla ore gen is enabled.** GregTech removing early game caving, even if it's strictly suboptimal compared to just nuking ore veins, is really boring. Vanilla ores are (if i can figure it out) blacklisted from the ore miners, so they shouldn't pollute ore mining output.\
Bedrock ore veins are enabled, though as of yet not properly implemented. Resource generation is one of the biggest pitfalls of GregTech as a mod. Unlike MI as mentioned in the introduction, there is simply no way to have a GregTech factory passively topped off with resources. There are some passive lines like the stone processing lines that can yield some resources, but this is very simplistic, does not cover everything you need, and probably scales poorly. Every modpack has a different approach to solving this, but I prefer using what the mod itself has as of right now.

**Exploding machines have been toned down**, weather/terrain does *not* cause explosions, and explosions do *not* damage the environment. This is another pain point for many people, and rightfully so. Weather interacting with machines this way is straight up just stupid, and overvolting causing explosions is a very harsh punishment for what is a relatively small mistake which can nuke a players motivation to continue playing if they arent prepared or dont have a recent backup. As is, the mechanic is still *present*, but it is as unpunishing as possible. The hope is that players are encouraged to prepare for these incidents with backup materials and such, rare though it may happen. Once a player unlocks AE its basically a nonissue.

**Food is a major part of survival Minecraft** that is generally shunned by the average GregTech modpack, though its not as if food as a mechanic is really interesting enough for me to view that behavior towards it as a bad thing. I have always enjoyed the idea of food in factory modpacks just being an extra problem you have to solve, while also reframing what your factory is actually *doing* (think biters in factorio). As of right now, Spice of Life: Carrot Edition adds the typical health buffs for a varied diet, but I have also added Spice of Life: Classic Edition, which adds the diminishing returns mechanic found in GTNH. This will surely need to be touched up overtime, and if the overwhelming consensus is that it sucks, i'll happily remove it or make it an opt in. I don't want this pack to be played on peaceful (not that this will be forced on players), so just doing that isn't an adequate way to opt out. Pams has been added to add food variety, though I don't have any particular attachment to any food mod. The only stipulation is that I only want one present (not including addons).

**Pollution and item hazards are enabled.** Item hazards are a relatively unexplored aspects of CEuM that generally just get disabled by pack devs or are invalidated through the use of AE2 logistics. I wouldn't call it an interesting mechanic, but I think it goes a long way for immersion, and can present some minor challenge in the early to mid game. Pollution is very much a controversial mechanic, though the CEuM is, to my knowledge, much less of a problem than what is present in GTNH. As a frame of reference, I ran a maxed out parallel EBF with the lowest tier muffler hatch for like half an hour and only the chunk that the multiblock resided in was actually affected by the pollution, so it seems to be isolated to the single chunk in which it is generated in. Again, something I will remove if particularly unpopular, but it seems as though the mechanic is just an incentive for some small passive power sinks.

**AE2 is obviously present** in the pack, despite my earlier comments about it, but it will likely be gated behind EV, or whenever GCYR space travel takes place in progression. GT logistics are a very overlooked aspect of the mod, generally because covers and pipes are so exorbitantly expensive.
